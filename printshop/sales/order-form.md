# Taking an order without losing money on it

A reprint costs you the whole margin. Almost every reprint traces back to a
detail you didn't capture at the start.

## Ask these, every time

1. **The exact text**, copied and pasted, not retyped by you. Capitals matter —
   ANNA and Anna are different products.
2. **Colour pair** — base and lettering. Offer three combinations, not a
   catalogue: black with gold, white with black, black with white.
3. **Quantity**, and whether each item has different text.
4. **Deadline.** "Before Christmas" is not a date. Get the date.
5. **Delivery** — post or collect, and the address if posting.
6. **For repairs:** a photo next to a ruler, what it belongs to, the model
   number if there is one, and whether it takes any weight.

## Confirm before you print

> Just to confirm before I start:
>
> - **Text:** ANNA (capitals, exactly as shown)
> - **Colours:** matte black plate, silk gold lettering
> - **Quantity:** 1
> - **Ready by:** Friday 3 October
> - **Delivery:** posted to [address]
> - **Total:** €22, payable [on collection / now]
>
> Anything wrong here, tell me now — once it's printed the name can't be changed.

That last line has saved more margin than any other sentence in this folder.

## The policies, stated up front

- **Personalised items can't be returned.** Say it in the listing and in the
  confirmation. It is also the law in most places, but say it anyway.
- **A typo you sent is a new order.** A typo I made is my reprint, free, same week.
- **Christmas cutoff: 10 December.** Published, and held. Late orders are how a
  good December becomes a miserable one.
- **Jobs start at €30.** Anything smaller is a favour, and favours get talked about.
- **Payment on order for anything over €50**, or you become an interest-free
  lender to strangers.

## After delivery

Two lines, sent the day after:

> Hope it turned out how you wanted. If you ever need a small plastic part —
> something broken you can't buy any more — that's the other half of what I do.

That message is how a €14 keychain buyer turns into a €60 repair customer.
