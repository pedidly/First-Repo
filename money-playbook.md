# The Money Playbook

Ranked by **time to first dollar**, not by how exciting it sounds. Every tier lists what
it actually pays, what it costs you to start, and the single next action.

Written August 2026. Rates move — every figure here is sourced and dated at the bottom,
and anything marked ⚠️ came from a low-quality source and should be verified before you
act on it.

**One rule before you read anything else:** an hour spent raising the rate on work you
already know how to do beats an hour of almost everything below. If you have a marketable
skill, Tier 2 is your answer and the rest is a distraction.

---

## Tier 0 — Money that is already yours (0–72 hours, $0 in)

This is the only tier with no downside. Do all four before touching anything else.

### 1. Unclaimed property search
States hold billions in forgotten deposits, final paychecks, insurance payouts, and closed
accounts. Searching is free, and any site charging a "finder's fee" is a middleman you
don't need.

- **Pays:** $0 to five figures. Most hits are $50–$500. Genuinely random.
- **Time to cash:** New York pays most simple claims within 30 days; complex or multi-owner
  claims run to 180 days; securities claims can take up to a year.
- **Do now:** Search `missingmoney.com` (run by the national association of state
  administrators, covers most states), then search your own state treasurer's site
  directly, then every state you've lived in. Use maiden names and old addresses.

### 2. Class action settlements you qualify for
You have almost certainly been a customer of a company that settled. Claims are usually a
web form and take five minutes.

- **Pays:** $5–$300 typically, occasionally much more with documented losses.
- **Time to cash:** 6–18 months. Slow, but the effort is near zero.
- **Do now:** Search open settlement trackers for products and services you've used in the
  last five years. File anything where you can honestly attest.

### 3. Subscription audit
The fastest guaranteed return in this document, because it's a *recurring* win.

- **Pays:** Median household finds $20–$80/month of genuinely unused subscriptions.
  Cancelling $50/month is worth the same as a $600/year raise, after tax, forever.
- **Time to cash:** Immediate.
- **Do now:** Open your bank and card statements, sort by merchant, and read the last three
  months line by line. Cancel anything you can't remember using. Then call your phone and
  internet providers and ask for the retention department — not the sales line.

### 4. Paycheck withholding check
If you get a large tax refund every year, you gave the government an interest-free loan.

- **Pays:** Converts a lump refund into $100–$400/month of cash flow now.
- **Do now:** Run the IRS withholding estimator, adjust your W-4 with HR. Note this is
  *timing*, not new money — but cash flow now is worth more than cash flow in April.

## Tier 1 — Cash this week (hours to 7 days, $0–100 in)

Low ceiling, high certainty. Use these to cover an actual gap, not as a strategy.

### 5. Sell what you already own
Still the fastest real money for most people.

- **Pays:** Highly variable, but a typical household clears $300–$2,000 in one weekend.
  Electronics, tools, bikes, instruments, and designer clothing move fastest.
- **Time to cash:** Same day on local marketplaces (cash in hand), 3–7 days shipped.
- **Do now:** Photograph ten items today. Price at 60% of the going used rate to sell in
  48 hours rather than 60 days. Local pickup avoids shipping and payment-hold friction.

### 6. Plasma donation
Real money, real time cost, and a real medical commitment. Not for everyone.

- **Pays:** Roughly **$30–$75 per visit**, twice weekly max. New-donor promotions are
  advertised as high as **$700–$1,100 for the first month** across ~8 donations. ⚠️ Those
  headline numbers come from affiliate sites that profit from referrals — the advertised
  maximum is a marketing figure with conditions attached. Assume the real number is lower.
- **Time to cash:** Same day, loaded to a prepaid card. First visit takes ~2 hours for
  screening; later visits ~1 hour.
- **Reality check:** ~$25–40/hour of your time for the first month, then it drops sharply
  once the new-donor bonus expires. It is a one-month arbitrage, not an income stream.
- **Do now:** Call your two nearest centers and ask what the *current* new-donor schedule
  is and what disqualifies you. Do not rely on any website's number, including this one.

### 7. Paid research studies and focus groups
Consistently the best-paid hour in this tier and the most overlooked.

- **Pays:** $50–$250 for a 60–90 minute session. UX research studies for software pay at
  the top of that range, and you are qualified for them if you use software.
- **Time to cash:** 1–14 days after the session.
- **Do now:** Register on two or three research-recruiting panels and university research
  boards near you. Fill out the screener honestly and completely — the screener is the
  whole game.

### 8. Delivery and rideshare gig work
The default answer everyone gives, and close to the worst-paying option here.

- **Pays:** Gross **$15–22/hour**. After vehicle costs ($5–8/hr) and self-employment tax
  ($2–3/hr), realistic take-home is **$7–13/hour**. ⚠️ You will find sites claiming $24–28/hr
  *net*; those are affiliate-driven and inconsistent with every conservative estimate.
  Believe the low number.
- **The hidden cost:** Vehicle depreciation is real money you pay later. At ~$0.65/mile of
  true operating cost, high-mileage delivery can be near-breakeven in a car you'll need to
  replace.
- **Verdict:** Use it as a liquidity bridge for a specific short gap. It has no ceiling,
  builds no asset, and every hour is sold once.
- **If you do it:** Weekend dinner peaks only, decline anything under ~$2/mile, and track
  every mile for the tax deduction.

### 9. Local skilled labor
Furniture assembly, moving help, hauling, yard work, pet sitting, tech setup for older
neighbors.

- **Pays:** $25–60/hour, cash, usually same day.
- **Why it beats delivery:** No vehicle depreciation, repeat customers, and you set price.
- **Do now:** Post in one neighborhood group with a specific service and a specific price.
  "I'll assemble your IKEA furniture, $60 flat" outperforms a general offer every time.

## Tier 2 — The real answer for anyone with a skill (1–4 weeks)

This is where the money actually is. Everything above is a rounding error next to it.

### 10. Freelance and contract work at your actual rate

- **Pays:** Market average for experienced freelance software developers in 2026 is roughly
  **$73/hour mid-level to $128/hour senior**, with specialists above $200/hour. On Upwork
  specifically, development work realistically clears **$60–100+/hour**, minus Upwork's flat
  **10% fee**.
- **Time to first dollar:** 1–3 weeks to a signed contract if you already have proof of work.
- **The math that matters:** One senior freelance hour ≈ **ten hours** of net delivery
  driving. If you have the skill, an hour spent finding a client is worth ten hours spent
  in the car — even if four out of five leads go nowhere.
- **Do now, in order:**
  1. Write down three problems you have actually solved end to end. Those are your services.
  2. Set your rate at market mid-point, not below. Underpricing filters for bad clients.
  3. Contact **ten people who already know you** before you touch any platform. Warm leads
     convert at an order of magnitude better than cold platform bids, and pay no fee.
  4. Only then, list on a platform — as a lead source, not a home.

### 11. Subcontract to an agency
Agencies are chronically over-sold and under-staffed. They pay less per hour than direct
clients but hand you continuous work with zero sales effort.

- **Pays:** 50–70% of the direct rate, with far more volume and no client management.
- **Do now:** Find five small agencies in your specialty, email the founder directly, and
  say you have capacity this month. That specific sentence gets answered.

### 12. Contract-to-hire and full-time roles
If your income problem is structural rather than a one-week gap, a better-paying role is
worth more than any side hustle in this document, compounding, forever.

- **Do now:** Apply to five roles at your level with a resume tuned to each. Five careful
  applications beat fifty generic ones.

## Tier 3 — Money that needs money (needs capital, low effort, capped)

Real returns, but strictly proportional to the cash you already have. None of these help
if the balance is zero.

### 13. Bank account sign-up bonuses
The highest risk-free return available to a normal person, if you can meet direct-deposit
requirements.

Representative offers live as of August 2026 — **verify before opening, most expire in
September–October 2026**:

| Bank | Bonus | Main requirement |
|---|---|---|
| Chase Total Checking | $400 | $1,000 in direct deposits within 90 days |
| BMO Smart Advantage / Smart Money | $400 | $4,000 in qualifying direct deposits in 90 days |
| Huntington Perks Checking | $400 | $500 in qualifying direct deposits in 90 days |
| KeyBank Smart Checking | $300 | $2,000 in eligible direct deposits in 90 days |
| SoFi Checking | up to $400 | direct deposit tiers |
| Capital One 360 Checking | $250 | two direct deposits of $500+ within 75 days |

- **Effective return:** A $400 bonus on a $1,000 direct-deposit requirement held 90 days is
  an absurd annualized rate. Nothing else here comes close.
- **Costs:** A hard or soft pull depending on bank, 30 minutes of setup, and the discipline
  to close the account after the holding period so fees don't eat the bonus.
- **Do now:** Pick the one whose direct-deposit requirement you can actually meet with your
  existing paycheck. Set a calendar reminder for the day the holding period ends.

### 14. High-yield savings
Not a strategy — just the correct place to keep cash you already have.

- **Pays:** Top accounts are around **4.10–4.21% APY** as of August 2026, against a national
  average savings rate of **0.38%**. On $10,000 that's roughly $400/year instead of $38.
  Rates are trending slightly downward.
- **Do now:** If your emergency fund is in a big-bank savings account, move it this week.
  This is a ten-minute task worth several hundred dollars a year, permanently. ⚠️ Ignore any
  site advertising savings APYs far above ~4.3%; those listings are usually promotional
  teasers, balance-capped, or not what they appear to be.

## Tier 4 — Compounding assets (3–12 months, low hit rate, high ceiling)

Only start here once Tiers 0–2 are handled. These pay nothing for months and then
occasionally pay for years.

### 15. Productize something you've already built
The cheapest product to build is the one that exists. In this very repository there's a
finished, dependency-free weather dashboard — that's a template, a licensable component, or
a paid niche tool, not just a prototype.

- **Pays:** Most attempts earn under $100. A few earn $500–5,000/month. The distribution is
  brutally skewed and honesty about that is the whole point of this section.
- **Do now:** Take one thing you built, write a one-page sales page describing the problem
  it solves, and put a price on it. Ship before it's ready.

### 16. Teaching what you know
Course, template pack, or written guide in a narrow niche you have real credibility in.

- **Reality:** Broad topics are saturated to worthlessness. Narrow ones still pay.
  "Weather data pipelines for small-craft operators" beats "learn to code."

### 17. Consulting retainers
The endgame of Tier 2. Convert your best freelance client into a fixed monthly retainer for
guaranteed availability.

- **Pays:** $2,000–10,000/month per retained client, and it's predictable income rather than
  project whiplash.
- **Do now:** Ask your best current or past client what they'd pay to have you on call.

## The anti-list — reliably loses money

Ranked by how confidently the evidence says to avoid them.

1. **Day trading and options.** The consistent finding across regulator and academic studies
   is that the large majority of active retail traders lose money net of costs, and
   losses scale with activity. It feels like skill and behaves like a fee.
2. **Sports betting and prediction markets as income.** Negative expected value by
   construction. The house edge is the product.
3. **Speculative crypto positions taken to solve a cash-flow problem.** Volatility is
   uncorrelated with your rent due date.
4. **MLM / "distributor" opportunities.** Income disclosures published by the companies
   themselves routinely show the median participant earning near zero or losing money.
5. **Dropshipping and Amazon FBA courses.** The course is the business model; you are the
   customer.
6. **"AI side hustle" course funnels.** Same structure, newer paint.
7. **Paid survey grinding.** Frequently under $3/hour of real time. Focus groups (#7) are a
   different thing entirely — take those, skip these.
8. **Any "opportunity" requiring upfront payment to start earning.** Legitimate work pays
   you; it does not invoice you.
9. **Payday and cash-advance apps.** Effective annualized rates in the triple digits. This
   is not making money, it's renting it at the worst price available.

## How to actually choose

Answer one question: **is this a gap or a trend?**

- **A one-time gap** (a bill this month) → Tier 0, then #5 sell things, then #7 research
  studies. Skip everything else. Do not restructure your life for a one-month problem.
- **A persistent shortfall** → Tier 2, exclusively. Raise your rate or change your role.
  Side hustles do not fix an income structure problem; they just spend the hours you'd need
  to fix it.
- **You have savings but they're idle** → Tier 3. Ten minutes of work, several hundred
  dollars a year.
- **You want an asset, not an hourly wage** → Tier 4, funded by Tier 2, and give it six
  months before judging it.

The honest summary: Tier 0 is free money and everyone should do it today. Tier 2 is the
real answer for anyone with a skill. Tier 1 is a bridge, not a plan. Tier 3 needs capital.
Tier 4 needs patience. And every single thing on the anti-list is more popular than every
single thing in Tier 0, which is precisely why the anti-list exists.
